# Discovery and Qualification

**Template version:** v1

**Purpose:** Find one measurable workflow that can be piloted within explicit system, human, approval, and evidence boundaries.

Do not use this guide to force a workflow into the offer. The correct outcome can be `Do not proceed`.

## 2. Preparation

Before the session:

- [ ] Review only customer-provided or approved background.
- [ ] Separate known facts from assumptions to test.
- [ ] Confirm who can explain the workflow step by step.
- [ ] Invite the business outcome owner, workflow owner, and an operator where possible.
- [ ] Prepare to capture exact customer language.
- [ ] Ask permission before recording or retaining identifiable quotes.
- [ ] Do not bring outcome, timeline, pricing, integration, or capability promises into the session.

## 3. Opening

Suggested internal script:

> We want to understand one piece of operational work before discussing a pilot. We will map how it works today, where it slows down, what must remain under human control, and whether there is a fair way to measure a change. If the workflow is not bounded or measurable enough, we will say so.

This is a conversation aid, not approved public copy.

- Which part causes the most operational friction?
- How often does it happen?

- What workarounds do people use?
- What costs or consequences are observed versus assumed?

### Human judgment and accountability

Ask:
- Which steps require judgment, authorization, empathy, negotiation, or legal/business accountability?
- Which actions could affect a person, customer, payment, access, record, commitment, or external system?
- Which decisions must remain human?
- What may an AI Employee observe, summarize, classify, draft, recommend, or execute?
- Who can approve each consequential action?
- What should happen when an approver rejects, does not respond, or is unavailable?
- What conditions should stop the workflow and route it to a person?

### Baseline and measurement

Ask:
- What would we measure before and during the pilot?
- How is that measure defined today?
- Where does the source data come from?
- Is the sample representative of normal work?
- What observation window avoids an unfair comparison?
- Who can validate the method and the source?
- What contextual changes could affect the result?
- What result would support `Stop`, `Refine`, or `Repeat`?

Candidate measures can include time, volume, completion, rework, exceptions, quality, or another customer-defined operational signal. Their inclusion is not a promise that the pilot will improve them.

## 6. Qualification Gates

Mark each row:

- `Pass` — named owner and adequate evidence exist.
- `Needs evidence` — plausible, but a fact, owner, access decision, or verification is missing.
- `Fail` — the condition cannot be met within the proposed pilot.

Do not average or score away a failed required gate.

## 7. Automatic `Do Not Proceed` Conditions

Do not proceed when:
- there is no accountable business or workflow owner;
- the request is for unbounded autonomy or removal of human accountability;
- a workflow cannot be separated from an undefined transformation program;
- no fair baseline or observation method can be established;
- required access lacks an authorized owner;
- consequential actions have no approver, deny path, stop condition, or manual fallback;
- the pilot depends on an unverified critical capability with no bounded validation path;
- the buyer requires a guaranteed outcome, unsupported runtime, or unapproved commercial commitment;
- representative evidence cannot be captured or validated.

A declined workflow may return to discovery if the missing condition materially changes.

### Decision rules

- **Proceed:** Every required gate is `Pass`; move the candidate into the pilot charter.
- **Needs evidence:** No gate is an irreversible `Fail`, but at least one is unresolved; assign owners and do not start the pilot.
- **Do not proceed:** At least one required gate cannot be met within a bounded pilot.

## 9. Discovery Handoff

Before creating the pilot charter, attach or reference:

- [ ] current-state workflow map;
- [ ] exact customer problem language;
- [ ] representative case references;
- [ ] qualification gate record;
- [ ] candidate baseline and measurement notes;
- [ ] initial human/AI boundary;
- [ ] systems/data/access list;
- [ ] named owners and approvers;
- [ ] assumptions and product-verification dependencies;
- [ ] decision record.

No discovery statement becomes public proof through this handoff.
