# One workflow. Clear boundaries. Evidence your team can review.

A vWorkspace pilot starts with a real piece of operational work—not a broad AI rollout. Together, we map how one workflow runs today, define what an AI Employee may and may not do, and agree how the pilot will be evaluated.

People remain accountable for sensitive or consequential actions.

## Start with the work

Bring one workflow that has:

- a clear trigger and completion point;
- a named business owner and people who perform the work today;
- an observable source for a baseline;
- systems and data with accountable owners;
- human approvers and a manual fallback.

If the workflow cannot be bounded, measured, or governed, it is not ready for a pilot.

## How the pilot works

**1. Discover the current workflow**

We map the steps, handoffs, exceptions, systems, and evidence behind the work. Assumptions stay labeled as assumptions.

**2. Agree the boundary**

We define what is in and out of scope, what the AI Employee may observe, draft, recommend, or execute, and what must remain human work.

**3. Verify before running**

Required access, product behavior, approval paths, exception handling, stop conditions, and the manual fallback are checked before pilot runs begin.

**4. Review the evidence**

Pilot observations are compared with the agreed baseline. Human approvals, interventions, exceptions, missing data, and limitations remain part of the record.

## Your team stays in control

- Named people approve or reject defined actions.
- Silence is not treated as approval.
- Ambiguous, rejected, timed-out, or out-of-scope work follows the agreed exception or manual path.
- Permissions and responsibilities are limited to the pilot workflow.
- Your evidence owner validates the baseline, observations, and limits.

## What the pilot should answer

- Is this workflow suitable for an AI Employee?
- Does the human/AI boundary work in practice?
- Do approvers receive enough context to make a decision?
- What changed, what did not, and what remains uncertain?
- Should the workflow stop, be refined, or be repeated?

The closeout is a decision supported by evidence—not a guaranteed result.
